    Module · prefix Events:

    Events and impacts

    The Events module represents disruptive events that may affect the operation of urban transport services, such as road accidents, roadworks, vehicle breakdowns and road closures. Its objective is to describe the nature of these events, their spatial and temporal extent, and their consequences for the transport system.

    The module distinguishes between the occurrence of an event and its resulting impacts. An event is characterized by its type, geographical location, starting time and ending time, while its impacts may concern road infrastructure, public transport stops, transport actors or passenger travel plans.

    The representation relies on the reuse of the Simple Event Model (SEM), the Transport Disruption Ontology (Dtx), the Event Ontology and OWL-Time.

    By connecting disruptive events with the Infrastructure, GTFS and DRT modules, this module supports the identification of affected road segments, transport stops and passenger reservations, providing a semantic foundation for disruption-aware transport planning and decision-making.

    The ontologies behind the module

    The Events module adds an impact layer to published ontologies. They are imported, not copied into this documentation: each card says what the ontology contains and what is used here, with a link to its official documentation. The diagram shows how they fit together.

    Simple Event Model (SEM)

    sem:

    A minimal model of events: what happened (sem:Event↗), who took part (sem:Actor↗), where (sem:Place↗) and when (sem:Time↗). Each can have a type, such as an event type (sem:EventType↗).

    In this ontology: the events of the knowledge graph are sem:Event↗s. Their type (sem:eventType↗), their time (sem:hasTime↗, sem:hasBeginTimeStamp↗, sem:hasEndTimeStamp↗), their places (sem:Place↗) and the actors they affect (sem:Actor↗) come from SEM. Events.rdf contains a complete copy of SEM (17 classes, 15 object properties, 7 datatype properties).

    Terms used here: sem:Actor↗ sem:Event↗ sem:eventType↗ sem:hasBeginTimeStamp↗ sem:hasEndTimeStamp↗ sem:hasTime↗ sem:Place↗ sem:Time↗

    W. R. van Hage, V. Malaisé, R. Segers, L. Hollink, G. Schreiber. Design and use of the Simple Event Model (SEM). Journal of Web Semantics 9(2), 2011.

    Official documentation ↗

    Event Ontology (c4dm)

    event:

    The Event Ontology of the Centre for Digital Music (Music Ontology project). Dtx defines its events as event:Event↗; the Events module declares event:Event↗ ⊑ sem:Event↗, so that Dtx events are SEM events too.

    In this ontology: event:Event↗ is the domain of HasImpact. The simulated events of the knowledge graph are both event:Event↗ and sem:Event↗.

    Terms used here: event:Event↗

    Y. Raimond, S. Abdallah. The Event Ontology, 2007.

    Transport Disruption ontology (Dtx)

    Dtx:

    An ontology of events that disrupt travel: a classification of transport event types taken from the European DATEX II standard, plus a small model linking events to their disruptive impacts (Dtx:DisruptiveImpact↗) and to the travel plans (Dtx:Plan↗) they affect.

    In this ontology: the type of each event is taken from the Dtx classification (table below); Dtx:DisruptiveImpact↗ is a subclass of impact; a DRT reservation is a Dtx:Plan↗.

    Dtx contains 426 classes. Its event types form three classifications, each with its own sub-branches (number of classes in brackets):

    ClassificationClassesMain branches
    Dtx:OperatorAction↗72Dtx:InfrastructureSideAssistance↗ (8), Dtx:InfrastructureWorks↗ (25), Dtx:NetworkManagement↗ (36)
    Dtx:PublicTransportEvent↗3Dtx:PublicTransportCancellation↗ (0), Dtx:PublicTransportDelay↗ (0), Dtx:PublicTransportDiversion↗ (0)
    Dtx:TrafficElementEvent↗345Dtx:AbnormalTraffic↗ (5), Dtx:Accident↗ (38), Dtx:Activity↗ (92), Dtx:Conditions↗ (93), Dtx:InfrastructureFailure↗ (22), Dtx:InfrastructureFault↗ (20), Dtx:Obstruction↗ (68)

    Besides the event types, Dtx has a small model of disruption: Dtx:DisruptiveImpact↗, Dtx:Plan↗ and the properties Dtx:causeOf↗, Dtx:hasCause↗, Dtx:hasPlan↗, Dtx:impactsOn↗, Dtx:relevantTo↗.

    OWL-Time

    time:

    W3C ontology of temporal concepts: instants (time:Instant↗), intervals (time:Interval↗) and their beginnings and ends.

    In this ontology: the DRT module uses OWL-Time intervals (time:Interval↗) for the pickup and drop-off windows of a reservation; in the knowledge graph, the time of each event is an interval with a beginning and an end (time:hasBeginning↗, time:hasEnd↗).

    Terms used here: time:hasBeginning↗ time:hasEnd↗ time:hasTime↗ time:Instant↗ time:Interval↗ time:inXSDDateTimeStamp↗
