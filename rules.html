    Application rules (SWRL)

    The Urban-Mobility-Ontology is a generic model of urban mobility and demand-responsive transport. In the YeloFlex project we applied it to a specific task: optimising the routes of autonomous on-demand shuttles in the La Rochelle area. For that application we wrote a few SWRL rules that classify reservations according to how hard they are to serve.

    These rules encode decisions of that application, such as the 20- and 30-minute thresholds below. They are not general truths about DRT, so they are kept out of the four modules and published separately in an application module that imports the DRT module. You can reuse the ontology without them, or adapt their thresholds to your own service.

    Why a separate module

    The DRT module defines four subclasses of Reservation: ShortNoticeReservation, TightPickupWindowReservation, TightDropWindowReservation and ComfortSensitiveReservation. The ontology only says what these classes mean. It does not say how short a notice must be to count as short, or how narrow a time window must be to count as tight: that depends on the service, its fleet and its operator.

    In our application, the routing optimiser needs to know which reservations constrain the routes most, so that it can schedule them first. The rules below fill the four classes from the data of each reservation, with thresholds chosen for the YeloFlex service. Keeping them in their own module means that the generic ontology carries no application choices, and that anyone can see exactly which inferences come from our application.

    How to use it

    • Generic use: import only https://w3id.org/urbanmobility/drt. The reservation classes are available, without any rule.
    • Reproducing our application: import https://w3id.org/urbanmobility/drt-rules, which imports the DRT module, and run a reasoner that supports SWRL with built-ins, such as Pellet or the SWRLTab and Drools engine of Protégé.
    • Your own application: copy the rules and change the thresholds, or write rules of your own in a separate module that imports the DRT module.

    All times are in seconds: 1,200 s = 20 minutes, 1,800 s = 30 minutes. The rules read the datatype properties noticeSec, pickupSlackSec, dropoffSlackSec and userProfile.

    The rules

    Each rule is written as body → head: when every atom of the body holds, the head is inferred. Variables start with ?.

    S10

    A reservation whose pickup slack is at most 1,200 seconds (20 minutes) is a tight pickup window reservation.

    DRT:Reservation(?r) ∧ DRT:pickupSlackSec(?r, ?s) ∧ swrlb:lessThanOrEqual↗(?s, 1200)
  → DRT:TightPickupWindowReservation(?r)
    S11

    A reservation whose notice time is greater than 0 and at most 1,200 seconds (20 minutes) is a short-notice reservation.

    swrlb:greaterThan↗(?n, 0) ∧ swrlb:lessThanOrEqual↗(?n, 1200) ∧ DRT:Reservation(?r) ∧ DRT:noticeSec(?r, ?n)
  → DRT:ShortNoticeReservation(?r)
    S12

    A reservation made by a customer whose profile is 'elderly' is a comfort-sensitive reservation.

    DRT:Reservation(?r) ∧ DRT:userProfile(?c, "elderly") ∧ DRT:madeBy(?r, ?c)
  → DRT:ComfortSensitiveReservation(?r)
    S13

    A reservation whose drop-off slack is greater than 0 and at most 1,800 seconds (30 minutes) is a tight drop-off window reservation.

    DRT:Reservation(?r) ∧ DRT:dropoffSlackSec(?r, ?ds) ∧ swrlb:greaterThan↗(?ds, 0) ∧ swrlb:lessThanOrEqual↗(?ds, 1800)
  → DRT:TightDropWindowReservation(?r)
    S14

    A reservation made by a customer whose profile is 'PMR' (person with reduced mobility) is a comfort-sensitive reservation.

    DRT:Reservation(?r) ∧ DRT:userProfile(?c, "PMR") ∧ DRT:madeBy(?r, ?c)
  → DRT:ComfortSensitiveReservation(?r)
